The goal is to understand the customer's concern and help them make an informed decision. Use these examples to practice—not as a script to force a sale.

Listen → Acknowledge → Clarify → Agree on a next step.
Use verified figures, respect the customer's decision and follow your dealership's approved process.

01 · START THE CONVERSATION

“I'm just looking.”

Ask

“Of course. Are you exploring options, or is there a particular vehicle you want to see?”

Practice response

“Take your time. I can point you toward the right section and give you some space.”

Next step: Offer useful directions. Ask whether they want help now or prefer to browse.

02 · MAKE THE PRICE CLEAR

“Just give me your best price.”

Ask

“Which vehicle are you considering, and would you like an itemized out-the-door quote?”

Practice response

“I can obtain a written quote showing the selling price and included charges, with any conditions explained.”

Next step: Get approved figures. Identify optional items separately and explain the assumptions behind the quote.

03 · UNDERSTAND THE BUDGET

“The payment is too high.”

Ask

“What monthly range feels comfortable, and what total budget do you want to stay within?”

Practice response

“Let's review the price, down payment, term and available rate together. We can also consider a different vehicle.”

Next step: Compare approved options with total cost and term visible. Do not promise approval or hide a higher total cost behind a longer term.

04 · FIND THE UNANSWERED QUESTION

“I need to think about it.”

Ask

“Absolutely. What would be most useful to get clear before you leave—the vehicle, the figures, or something else?”

Practice response

“Take the time you need. I can put the information in writing so you have something clear to review.”

Next step: Answer the actual concern, then ask whether and when a follow-up would be helpful. Accept a decision to leave.

05 · INCLUDE THE OTHER DECISION-MAKER

“I need to talk to my partner.”

Ask

“Of course. What information would help the two of you review this together?”

Practice response

“I can send the vehicle details and written figures, or arrange a time for you both to see the vehicle.”

Next step: Agree on a useful handoff and ask permission before following up. Do not pressure them to decide without their partner.

A five-minute practice drill

Pick one objection. Have a partner play the customer. Acknowledge their concern, ask one clarifying question and agree on one useful next step. Swap roles. Your coach checks whether you listened, used accurate information and respected the customer's choice.
